Teachers get pitched a lot of AI tools. Most are a generic chatbot with a school-themed landing page. This guide keeps the ones that solve an actual classroom problem — planning, differentiation, writing practice and feedback — and shows what they cost when you pay as an individual teacher, not as a district.

1. MagicSchool — the best place to start

Free forever, or Plus at $8.33/month billed yearly. MagicSchool has 80+ tools built specifically for lesson plans, rubrics, quizzes, worksheets, IEP goal language and behaviour documentation. The free tier is genuinely usable; Plus removes generation caps, adds unlimited output history and gives access to student tools.

Where it fails: Outputs can be generic. A 30-second pass anchoring the prompt to your actual class makes a big difference. Some integrations (SIS/LMS, SSO) are Enterprise-only.

Verdict: Start here. Upgrade only if you hit limits during report season.

2. Khanmigo — free for teachers

Free for eligible educators. Khan Academy's teaching assistant covers differentiation, lesson plans, quiz questions, student groupings, hooks, exit tickets and rubrics. There's no teacher paid tier — the $4/month / $44/year plan is for learners and families.

Where it fails: Eligibility is restricted by country and age (18+). Its strength is tied to Khan Academy's content library, so it works best if that matches your curriculum.

3. Diffit — differentiation without the prep

Free Basic, or $14.99/month / $149.99/year. Paste an article or topic and Diffit rewrites it at several reading levels, with vocabulary and comprehension questions. Editable, printable and shareable.

The free catch: Basic only works with content created in the last 90 days. Paid adds unlimited resources, longer inputs, chapter books, standards alignment and exports to Google Docs, Slides, Forms and Classroom.

Where it fails: No student logins — teachers create materials, students use them offline or via your LMS. That's a feature for privacy, but a limitation if you want live student interaction.

4. Curipod — whole-class writing practice

Free plan with weekly live sessions and unlimited students. Curipod gets every student writing and responding, with AI feedback and no student accounts needed. Paid is school/district quote-only, so an individual teacher rarely needs to pay.

Where it fails: Free student responses are capped at 1,000 characters, and the weekly session allowance is limited.

5. ChatGPT Plus — the generalist

$20/month. Useful for parent communications, admin documents, sub plans and anything outside a dedicated teacher tool. Some free educator programmes have also been offered for verified US K-12 teachers — check current availability before paying.

Where it fails: No classroom privacy controls, no standards alignment and no student safeguards. Don't paste student names or work into a general chatbot.

6. Grammarly — quiet insurance

Free plan is usually enough. Newsletters, parent emails and handouts benefit from a proofreader. Pro adds tone adjustment, full-sentence rewrites and plagiarism detection at $12/month billed yearly.

Three rules before you use any of this with a class

  1. Never paste student names, work or IEP details into a general AI tool. Use purpose-built platforms with a privacy agreement, or anonymise everything.
  2. Check the output before it reaches a student. AI-generated worksheets include wrong answers more often than people expect.
  3. Check your school's policy. Some districts block specific tools, and some provide licences you already have — ask before paying.

FAQ

Are these tools really free for teachers?

MagicSchool, Khanmigo (eligible educators), Diffit Basic and Curipod all have real free tiers. Not 7-day trials in disguise. Limits apply, but they're workable.

Do I need to pay for anything?

Usually not. Start with the $0 stack for a full term. Pay only when a specific limit costs you time every week.

Which is best for lesson plans?

MagicSchool is purpose-built for it. ChatGPT is more flexible but needs more prompting and gives no privacy controls.

What about student data?

Dedicated education platforms are the safe option. General-purpose AI assistants should never receive identifiable student information. When in doubt, anonymise.
